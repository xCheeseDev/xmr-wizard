    Questions, answered

    Who is this site for?

    This site is for anyone who wants to share control of Monero — digital cash — with other people, so no single person can spend it alone. Think business partners protecting a shared treasury, friends pooling money, or a family setting things up so two people must agree before anything moves.

    Normally that setup means everyone wrestling with terminal commands and passing long codes around in exactly the right order, where one typo ruins the whole thing. This site walks the entire group through it step by step: it tells each person what to type, checks the codes they paste in, and keeps track of whose turn it is until every wallet agrees the setup is complete. It never sees anyone’s private keys — only the public codes people share with each other.

    What is a Monero multisig wallet?

    It's a wallet that needs M out of N people to agree before any funds move — for example, 2-of-3 means any two of the three participants must sign. No single person can spend alone, and the wallet survives lost keys as long as enough participants remain.

    Does this site ever see my private keys?

    Never. This page is stateless: everything secret happens inside monero-wallet-cli on your own computer. The only things you paste here are the public coordination strings you were going to share with your co-signers anyway.

    What are the ceremony steps actually doing?

    prepare_multisig creates your personal shareable string; make_multisig combines everyone's strings into the shared wallet; the export/import rounds sync wallet data between participants until every wallet reports the setup is complete.

    Why are there multiple export/import rounds?

    Each round folds another participant's wallet info into everyone else's. Your wallet tells you when it's done — if it still asks for another round, there's info it hasn't absorbed yet.

    What happens if someone loses their key?

    That's exactly what M-of-N protects against: as long as at least M participants still have working keys, the funds can be spent. Pick M to survive the losses you realistically expect.

    Do we all have to be online at the same time?

    No — the ceremony is turn-based. Each person runs their command and shares their string whenever suits them (chat, email, QR code), and the group moves to the next step once everyone's pieces are in.

    Questions, answered

    Who is the spend coordinator for?

    Anyone who needs to move funds out of a multisig wallet. One person proposes the transaction, each required signer adds their signature, and anyone broadcasts it once enough signatures are collected — this page keeps the whole group in step.

    Do all signers have to be online at the same time?

    No — spending is turn-based, just like setup. Each signer runs their command and passes the transaction file along whenever suits them.

    What if one signer is unavailable?

    That’s what the threshold is for. In a 2-of-3 wallet, if one signer is away, the other two can still complete the spend — just skip the missing signer and pass the file to the next one.

    Does this site see my transaction or private keys?

    Never. Everything secret happens inside monero-wallet-cli on each signer’s own computer. This page only coordinates the order of steps and counts signatures.

    Does the signing order matter?

    The proposer always goes first, since they create the transaction. After that, the remaining signers can sign in any order your group agrees on.

    What happens after I broadcast?

    The transaction is submitted to the Monero network, and once it confirms, the funds have moved. Hit ‘Plan another spend’ to coordinate the next one.
